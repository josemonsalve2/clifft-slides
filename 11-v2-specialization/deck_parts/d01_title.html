Four generations of GPU backend. Three of them lost to a bytecode interpreter. This is what it took for the fourth to win — and what the losses taught.

One rule governs this deck

Trust data, not text
A fact is verified only if it can be re-derived from a committed artifact — a CSV, a summary.json, a SLURM log, an .hsaco — or from a file+line in the source tree. Prose in a design doc is a claim, not a fact.

The clifft tree carries months of design docs, plans, post-mortems and header comments. Much is accurate. Some is stale. A little is simply wrong — written from a hypothesis that measurement later refuted, and never deleted. Three findings from the audit contradict documents still in the tree:

V2 does not emit MLIR
It emits C. The directory is still called mlir/v2/ for historical reasons, and that name is the only MLIR left in it.
V1 authored zero MLIR passes
It ran three stock passes. One of them was a provable no-op on its input.
V2's one “loss” ran the wrong kernel
Its specialization failed the correctness gate and fell back to the interpreter. An earlier draft of the audit itself got this wrong.
Audit committed as V2_performance/VERIFIED_FACTS.md; every section cites it. Two facts could not be re-measured and are marked [unverified] in place rather than asserted.

Three of four generations tried per-circuit compilation. Two of those lost to an interpreter that compiles once and ships. Per-circuit compilation is not, by itself, a performance strategy — what you specialize matters more than that you specialize.

Why one job, one node, matters this much

The rule
mi350x-es is heterogeneous. Ratios are only sound when both arms ran in the same job on the same node. Absolute microseconds never transfer.
What happened when it was broken
An earlier draft projected ~0.40 for the circuit_d5 family by chaining an interpreter→specializer gain measured on d13-21 onto an SVM baseline measured on f13-21.

The direct measurement says ~0.84. Wrong by a factor of two, in the flattering direction.
The truncation that flattered
Job 50785 ran only 18 of 26 circuits: eight fixture paths had gone stale, aborted silently, and the harness reported “wins 18/18” over the truncated set.

The eight it dropped included all six QV circuits — the corpus's weakest wins. Median moved 0.670 → 0.518. A better-looking number produced entirely by missing data.
The fix
bench_all.sh:127-135 now aborts the run on a missing fixture rather than continuing past it. A benchmark that silently measures less than it claims is worse than one that fails.
Both errors moved the result in the flattering direction. That is not a coincidence — it is selection pressure. Errors that make results look worse get investigated immediately; errors that make them look better get published.
